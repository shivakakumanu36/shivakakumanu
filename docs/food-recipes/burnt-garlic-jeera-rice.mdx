This version of jeera rice leans into browned garlic for a nutty depth; a single portion keeps the garlic-to-rice ratio balanced so the flavor feels toasted but not bitter.

## Ingredients
- ½ cup basmati rice, rinsed
- 1 tablespoon ghee or oil
- ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
- 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- ½ green chili, slit lengthwise
- 1 cup water
- Salt to taste
- Fresh cilantro leaves for garnish

### 1. Prep the rice
- Soak the rice for 15 minutes, drain thoroughly, and keep it covered while you prep the aromatics.

### 2. Toast the aromatics
- Heat the ghee in a saucepan over medium heat, add cumin seeds, and let them sizzle before adding the garlic slices.
- Fry the garlic until it turns deep golden and fragrant, stirring constantly to avoid burning; toss in the chili for a quick flash.

### 3. Cook the rice
- Add the drained rice and salt to the pan, stirring until every grain is coated with the oil-infused aromatics.
- Pour in the water, bring to a boil, cover, lower the heat, and simmer for 12 minutes.
- Let it rest for 4 minutes with the lid on before fluffing and garnishing with cilantro.

## Chef’s Notes
- Keep an eye on the garlic; it should darken but not char, otherwise the smoke turns bitter.
- Leftover rice can be reheated with a splash of ghee for the same toasted finish.

> **Rice baseline:** I always track rice as ⅓ cup raw (≈67 g), which cooks to roughly 160 g; the calories table uses that cooked weight.
